Logline: A visionary designer battles the skepticism of industry and the limitations of mass production to bring beauty and functionality to everyday objects, transforming the lives of millions and shaping the modern world.

Three-Act Plot Outline:

Act 1: The Visionary's Spark
*   Introduction to Walter Dorwin Teague: In the early 20th century, Walter Dorwin Teague, a talented but unfulfilled illustrator, dreams of a world where everyday objects are not just functional but also beautiful and accessible to everyone. He sees the potential for art and industry to merge, creating a new field: industrial design.
*   The Birth of a Profession: Teague faces initial resistance from both traditional artists, who view mass production as vulgar, and industrialists, who prioritize cost over aesthetics. He struggles to articulate his vision of "visible rightness" and "heirlooms out of mass-produced manufactured objects."
*   Early Breakthroughs: Through sheer determination, Teague secures challenging early projects, proving the commercial and aesthetic value of his approach. He begins to attract a small team of like-minded individuals, laying the groundwork for his pioneering design firm.

Act 2: Shaping the Modern World
*   Rising Influence and Iconic Designs: Teague's reputation grows, leading to major contracts with prominent companies like Eastman Kodak. He designs iconic products, such as the Bantam Special camera, that become household names, solidifying his firm's position at the forefront of industrial design.
*   Challenges and Competition: As his influence expands, Teague navigates the complexities of corporate demands, balancing artistic integrity with commercial viability. He faces competition from emerging designers and skepticism from executives who struggle to fully grasp the long-term impact of good design.
*   The World's Fair Triumph: Teague's vision culminates in his groundbreaking exhibition designs for the 1939–40 New York World's Fair, including the Ford Building. These designs showcase his modern aesthetic to millions, cementing his status as the "Dean of Industrial Design" and demonstrating the power of design to shape public perception and desire.

Act 3: The Enduring Legacy
*   Passing the Torch and Evolution: As Teague ages, he reflects on his career and the evolving landscape of design and technology. He mentors a new generation of designers, ensuring his firm continues to innovate while staying true to his foundational principles of accessible beauty and functionality.
*   Personal Reflections and Triumphs: The film touches upon the personal sacrifices made for his dedication to his craft, but ultimately celebrates his profound achievements and the lasting impact of his work on American culture and everyday life.
*   A Designed Future: The movie concludes by showcasing the enduring influence of Teague's philosophy and designs, illustrating how his pioneering efforts transformed mass-produced goods into cherished objects, fulfilling his dream of creating "heirlooms out of mass-produced manufactured objects" for the masses, and leaving an indelible mark on the modern world.
